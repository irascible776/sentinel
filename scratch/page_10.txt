Pillar VI: Network & Infrastructure Threat Intelligence Threat intelligence is incomplete without analyzing the network communications and lateral movement patterns of deployed infrastructure hosting AI and enterprise workloads. 1. Network Flow Anomaly Detection (NetFlow / CICIDS) Sentinel processes high-throughput network flow logs (e.g., NetFlow v9, IPFIX, pcap statistics) to detect exfiltration, botnet command-and-control C2 communication, and volumetric denial-of-service attempts. ● Architecture: Autoencoders ( nn.Module with symmetric Encoder-Decoder linear layers and GELU activations). ● Mechanism: The network trains strictly on benign traffic flows, compressing $M$-dimensional netflow vectors into a lower-dimensional latent space. During inference, incoming traffic vectors are reconstructed. High Reconstruction Loss Mean Squared Error between input and reconstructed output) indicates anomalous network behavior and potential compromise. 2. Graph Neural Networks (GNN) for Lateral Movement Detection Adversaries navigating enterprise networks perform subtle host-to-host lateral movement that evades per-host security logging. ● Graph Formulation: The network is modeled as a dynamic heterogeneous graph $G = V, E$, where nodes $V$ represent host IP addresses, user accounts, and service endpoints, and directed edges $E$ represent authenticated connections, session creations, and network traffic flows. ● Architecture: Graph Convolutional Networks GCN or Graph Attention Networks GAT aggregate neighborhood feature representations. ● Inference: Node-level and link-prediction heads detect anomalous connection attempts—such as an unprivileged service account unexpectedly authenticating to a domain controller or AI infrastructure cluster—flagging active lateral movement in real time. Primary Datasets & Frameworks ● Datasets: CICIDS2018 , UNSWNB15 , LANL Cyber Security Dataset . ● Frameworks: PyTorch Geometric PyG, scikit-learn , dpkt , pyshark .
